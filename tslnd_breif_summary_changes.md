

**Changes from the base (test2\_updated.py) to solution logic that help find better solutions**

**1\. PPLN reduces the search space by up to 90%**The base fed the full QUBO to SA every time. PPLN first finds the smallest feasible subset of CDCs, then rebuilds InstanceData with only those CDCs. For your instance this cuts the variable count from 210 to 21. SA explores a landscape with 2²¹ states instead of 2²¹⁰ — exponentially easier to find a feasible minimum in.

**2\. Slack variables make constraints exact**The base used soft inequality penalties for constraints (14) and (15). This means a solution where demand > supply by 1 unit is penalised only proportionally — SA can still prefer it if the objective savings are large enough. The new formulation introduces binary slack variables so constraints become exact equalities. There is zero energy reward for violating them by even 1 unit. SA is pushed strictly toward the feasible region.

**3\. APL with bounded penalties keeps the energy landscape navigable**The base had no upper bound on penalties. After several iterations of violations, P1 could grow to values orders of magnitude larger than the objective terms, making every solution look nearly identical in energy. SA then performs a random walk rather than an informed search. The new P\_max = 500 × obj\_scale ceiling keeps the objective terms meaningful throughout all iterations so SA continues to distinguish between good and bad solutions even while enforcing constraints.

**4\. Greedy seed gives SA a near-feasible starting point**The base started every read from a random binary vector. In a 210-variable space most random starts are deeply infeasible and SA spends most of its budget just climbing toward feasibility. The greedy seed constructs a logically consistent starting assignment (each RDC assigned to its cheapest CDC, each active CDC assigned its cheapest plant, all slacks at 0). SA starts close to the feasible region and can immediately focus on objective improvement rather than constraint repair.
